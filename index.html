Zero

A personal debt-payoff planner. One household, one computer, no servers.

Zero works out what to pay on each debt this month so that the whole lot is gone as early as possible, and shows what that costs in interest against paying the minimums. It is built for the person who owns it and their family. It is not a service, there is nothing to sign up for, and nobody else operates it.

Why it asks for your bank data

A payoff plan is only as current as the figures behind it. Typing a balance, a rate, a minimum payment and a due date for every account, every month, is the reason people stop using tools like this. So Zero can read them from the accounts you choose to connect:

  • Balances — what is actually owed today.
  • Interest rates, minimum payments and due dates — the numbers the plan is computed from. These are the ones that matter: a rate you did not notice changing re-orders everything.
  • Transactions — so spending, and the fourteen-day cash forecast, are real rather than estimated.

Connecting is done through Plaid, who fetch the data from your institution on your behalf. You sign in on your own bank's page; Zero never sees that page and never sees your banking password. What Plaid does with your information is governed by their end user privacy policy.

What Zero does not do

  • It never moves money. No transfers, no payments, no trades. It tells you what to pay; you pay it.
  • It has no servers. Your data is stored on your own computer and sent nowhere. There is no account, no cloud copy, and no analytics.
  • It never stores a banking credential. Not the password, not a PIN. The access token from Plaid lives in your operating system's credential store.

How your data is protected

  • The application listens only on your own machine's loopback address, and that is fixed when it is built rather than configurable — so it cannot be exposed to a network by mistake.
  • Everything it stores is encrypted with a key derived from your passphrase, which is not kept anywhere. Until you unlock it, Zero cannot read its own files, and neither can anything else running on the computer.
  • Opening it needs two things: that passphrase, and a six-digit code from your authenticator app. Nothing is served and no bank can be connected until both are set up.
  • One command disconnects every institution at Plaid and deletes everything — the connection stops as well as the file.

The full detail is in the privacy policy.
